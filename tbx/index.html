Your station. Your signal. Your broadcast.

TBX turns the movies and shows you already own into live, scheduled television. Numbered channels, commercial breaks, a guide, and static between stations, on any TV, including the CRT in the basement. It is a small box that behaves like an appliance: you turn it on and it is already broadcasting.

01  Power on

No logos. No desktop. Just the station coming on the air.

Power the box and the screen stays black until the startup bumper lights the mark, arc by arc. Then TBX tunes back to the channel you were last watching, joining whatever is on right where the schedule says it should be.

02The idea

Flip, don't scroll.

Streaming made everything available and made watching feel like homework. You open an app, scroll rows of posters, and half the time you close it again without choosing anything.

Broadcast TV never asked you to choose. Something was always on. You flipped until something caught you, and the flipping was part of the fun: the static between stations, the commercial you half remember, the show already twenty minutes in.

TBX brings that back using the library you already have. You arrange your media into numbered channels with schedules, and TBX runs them like a station, around the clock, whether or not anyone is watching. Tune in and you land mid-programme. Flip away and it keeps going without you.

It is built to feel like a piece of home electronics rather than software: it boots straight to picture, it is set up from your phone, and it looks right on a thirty-year-old television.

05Signal Garden

Channel 99 is a place.

Tune to 99 and your Pip walks out of the TV into Signal Garden: a little village the whole family shares on one box. Grow bonsai, build and furnish a house, fish the pond, explore the caves under the village and trade at the market. Everything is played with the TV remote, and it all lives on the box: no accounts, no cloud.

A week in the garden Built around the family’s real week, so there’s always something to come back for.

  • Sunday morningCrackle the Static Peddler rolls in with jars of charged static.
  • All weekEvery Pip’s channel pays its own price for static, changing at noon. Mail jars to whoever’s price is climbing.
  • SaturdayRace day, and the last day to sell before the TV signs off for the week.
  • Any dayStalls restock, a Wandering Tinker may pass through, the trees need care, and the fish are biting.

09Brand

The glow.

The mark is a quarter of a rainbow rising from a corner: a signal spreading outward, and a nod to the colour bars and sign-on cards that used to fill the screen before the day's programmes began. The wordmark is lowercase because the box lives in a living room.

Logo versions Always use the supplied artwork; never retype the wordmark.

Construction Measured in units of a 168-unit square.

Five shapes share one centre, the bottom-right corner of the square. Four arcs are 25 units thick with 11-unit gaps between them; the aqua arc sits directly on the signal-blue quarter-disk.

In the lockup the wordmark stands 0.21 × the mark's height to its right. Its baseline sits on the mark's flat bottom and its ascenders reach the top. It is DM Sans SemiBold, tightened to −0.035 em, and supplied as outlines.

Glow palette Coral is the accent. Text on Coral is always Midnight.

10Patterns

Built from one shape.

Turn the mark around its corner four times and the arcs close into full rings. Stack half-rings and they become a scallop. Every pattern below is generated from the same geometry as the logo, for packaging, merchandise, the box's panels and the guide channel.

11Status

Where it stands today.

TBX is a working prototype. It runs every day on a Raspberry Pi 5 connected to a CRT, and the live demo is that same box.

Working today

  • Boots straight to broadcastWorking

    Black screen, startup bumper, then the last channel you watched.

  • Scheduled channelsWorking

    Day-part schedules, three playback orders, and tuning that joins shows in progress.

  • A station's in-between momentsWorking

    Commercial breaks, idents, station IDs, midnight and sign-off bumpers, static on empty channels.

  • Guide channel and PipsWorking

    A live guide on 00 with theme music, hosted by your Pip.

  • Signal Garden and the Seven WorldsPlayable

    Channel 99: the village, the market, the caves, and Pip Flyer’s seven worlds, all playable on the prototype. Being tuned through family play.

  • Control panel and phone remoteWorking

    Channels, schedules, media and setup wizards from any browser at home.

  • Getting media inWorking

    Network-share drop-in with automatic filing and conversion, a DVD ripping wizard and a PC converter.

  • Stays on the airWorking

    If the player stops, a watchdog restarts it in about two seconds and retunes.
