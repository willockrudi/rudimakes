How a repair works

Five steps, no surprises.

  1. 1

    Reach out

    Email the make, model and symptoms. I’ll tell you if I can help and give you a rough timeline.

  2. 2

    Drop off or ship

    Bring it to the shop by appointment, or ship it in. I do an intake and confirm the issue.

  3. 3

    Diagnosis

    I trace the fault, then send findings, options and an estimate before any repair work begins.

  4. 4

    Repair & test

    Once you approve, I fix it, test it properly and document everything. Usually 3–7 days.

  5. 5

    Pickup

    You get your gear back with a written summary and a 30-day labor warranty.

  • Real diagnostics. I trace the actual fault before I touch anything.
  • Straight pricing. $75 bench fee applied to the repair, published labor rates.
  • Your gear stays here. No farming out. It’s on my bench start to finish.

FAQ

Common questions.

How much does a repair cost?

All repairs start with a $75 bench/diagnostic fee ($50 for pedals), which gets applied to your total if you proceed. Labor is billed at published hourly rates - $85/hr for pedals and small electronics, $105/hr for amplifiers, $125/hr for synths and pro audio - plus parts at cost + 15%. I always confirm the estimate before starting. See full pricing.

How long does a repair take?

Most repairs are completed in 3–7 business days. Complex jobs or parts-dependent repairs may take longer - I'll give you a timeline estimate at diagnosis. Rush service (48-hour turnaround) is available for an additional $75.

What brands do you work on?

All of them. Fender, Marshall, Vox, Mesa/Boogie, Orange, Peavey, Ampeg - and boutique or vintage amps of any make. For synths: Moog, Roland, Korg, Sequential, Yamaha, Oberheim, and more. If it has circuits, bring it in.

Do you work on solid-state amps?

Yes. While tube amps are my specialty, I troubleshoot and repair solid-state amplifiers too. Same diagnostic approach, same documentation.

Can I ship my gear to you?

Yes - I take mail-in work from anywhere. Plenty of the gear on my bench arrives in a box. You pay shipping both ways; everything else works exactly like a local job: I diagnose it, send you findings and a written estimate, and nothing gets repaired until you approve it. Email me before you ship so I know it is coming and can tell you how to pack it.

How should I pack an amp for shipping?

Pull the tubes and the reverb tank and pack them separately - they are what break in transit. Double-box if you can, with at least two inches of padding on every side, and insure it for what it is actually worth. Email me first and I will tell you what to watch for on your specific amp. If it is a heavy head or a combo, freight is often cheaper than parcel, and I can point you at the options.

Do you take international work?

I will look at anything. Be aware that customs, insurance, and return freight on a heavy amp often cost more than the repair does, so for large pieces it is usually not worth it from overseas. Smaller things - pedals, modules, voice boards, synth chip sets - ship affordably and I take those from anywhere. Email me and I will give you a straight answer on whether it makes sense before you spend anything.

What if my gear isn't worth repairing?

I'll tell you. If the cost of repair exceeds the value of the gear, I'll let you know upfront so you can make an informed decision. You only pay the bench fee for the diagnosis.
