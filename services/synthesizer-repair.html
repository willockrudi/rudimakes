Synthesizer Repair in Indianapolis

Vintage synths fail at the component level, and the parts that fail are often the ones nobody stocks any more. That is the part most shops will not take on - it means sourcing, testing, and calibrating rather than ordering a board and dropping it in.

I work against service manuals and, where it helps, against a known-good machine on the same bench. Comparing a suspect reading to a healthy one under identical conditions is frequently the only way a drifted part gives itself away.

Common Faults

What Usually Comes In

01

Dead or detuned voices

On a Juno-106 that usually means the 80017A voice chips, which fail predictably with age. A full set replacement plus factory calibration brings the whole machine back into spec.

02

Noisy chorus

The bucket-brigade delay chips get noisier as they age. Verified against the surrounding circuit first - power rails, bias, LFO on the scope - so parts are not swapped on a guess.

03

Drifted calibration

Filter tracking, VCA offset, oscillator tuning, and noise level all drift. Full factory calibration in the sequence the service notes specify, not a quick trim.

04

Oscillator tuning

Analog oscillators that will not stay in tune or track across the keyboard - scaling, high-end trim, and temperature stability.

05

Key contacts and sliders

Unresponsive or double-triggering keys, scratchy sliders, and dead panel controls.

06

Power supplies

Rails out of spec cause faults everywhere downstream and get mistaken for a dozen other problems. Always checked before anything else.

07

Digital workstations

Factory resets, OS and sound data reloads, and boot failures on machines like the Triton where the original media is long gone.

Note: If your synth has a backup battery and stored patches you care about, back them up before the battery is replaced - swapping it wipes the memory.

FAQ

Common Questions

Do you work on vintage and modern synths?

Both. Moog, Roland, Korg, Sequential, Yamaha, Oberheim, and modern gear too. Vintage work is where the component-level sourcing matters most.

Can you source obsolete chips?

Often, yes. That is a normal part of vintage synth work - finding a correct or genuinely equivalent part, testing it, and calibrating around it.

Will a repair change how it sounds?

The goal is factory specification, not a reinterpretation. Where a repair genuinely improves on the original - a quieter chorus, for instance - I tell you what changed and why.

Can I ship a synth to you?

Yes, and small items like voice boards, modules, and chip sets ship cheaply from anywhere. Email me before sending anything.
