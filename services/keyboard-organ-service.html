Hammond, Leslie & Rhodes Service in Indianapolis

Electromechanical keyboards are half electronics and half machine, and they need someone willing to work on both. A Leslie that has lost its speed is a mechanical problem. A Hammond that hums is an electronic one. A Rhodes that plays unevenly is neither - it is geometry.

These instruments are old enough that maintenance has usually been deferred for decades. Most of what they need is not exotic, it is just overdue.

Common Faults

What Usually Comes In

01

Hammond hum

A 120 Hz hum through the AO-28 is the classic sign that the power supply filter capacitors are finished. Recapping it is routine, well-documented work.

02

Leslie speed and noise

Dry bearings and glazed drive wheels are why a Leslie will not come up to speed or will not hold one. Motor service, lubrication, and drive surface work.

03

Rhodes tuning and voicing

Tine height, escapement, and pickup position determine both tuning and bark. Setting them across the whole instrument is what makes a Rhodes play evenly.

04

Rhodes electronics

Suitcase preamps and power amps, including hard-to-source parts in the Peterson power amp that are usually written off as unobtainable.

05

Dead keys and contacts

Contact cleaning and adjustment on organs and electric pianos where keys are silent, weak, or inconsistent.

Note: These instruments are heavy and awkward, and a Leslie in particular does not travel well. If you are local, drop-off is usually the better call.

FAQ

Common Questions

Do you service Leslie cabinets?

Yes - motor service, bearings, drive wheels, lubrication, and the electronics.

Can you tune and voice a Rhodes?

Yes. Full tuning, tine and escapement setup, and pickup positioning across the instrument, not just the notes that are obviously off.

Do you do pickup and repair?

For heavy instruments locally, that can usually be arranged. Ask when you get in touch.

Is a full recap worth it on a Hammond?

On an untouched AO-28, generally yes. The capacitors are decades past their service life and the hum they cause will only get worse.
