Tube Amp Repair in Indianapolis

Tube amps fail in ways that are easy to guess at and expensive to guess wrong about. A hum can be filter capacitors, a heater fault, a bad ground, or a tube that is only bad under load. Swapping parts until it goes away is how a cheap repair turns into a bill for parts you did not need.

I diagnose first. The amp goes on the bench, I trace the actual fault, and you get findings and a written estimate before any repair work happens. Every job gets documented - what came in, what it turned out to be, and what fixed it.

Common Faults

What Usually Comes In

01

Hum and buzz

Filter capacitors dry out and stop doing their job, and the amp starts amplifying its own power supply ripple. On a recent Deluxe Reverb this showed up first as a blubbery low end under load, then as loud hum at idle.

02

Dead or weak output

Screen resistors, output tubes, and the high-voltage supply are the usual suspects. These are diagnosed against actual voltage readings, not by swapping tubes and hoping.

03

Crackle and intermittents

The hardest category, because the fault has to be present to be found. Dirty pots and jacks, cold solder joints, failing plate resistors, and tube sockets that have lost tension.

04

Bias drift and retubes

New tubes need the bias set for them. An amp running hot eats tubes and output transformers; running cold sounds thin and crossover-distorted.

05

Dead reverb or tremolo

Usually the recovery tube, the tank, or the RCA cables to it - all cheap, all commonly misdiagnosed as something worse.

06

Full recap and restoration

Electrolytics have a service life. On a vintage or hard-used amp, a full recap resets the clock and is often the difference between a reliable amp and one you cannot trust at a gig.

Note: Tube amps hold lethal voltage in the filter capacitors after they are unplugged. If you are not set up to discharge and measure them safely, this is not a good first repair to learn on.
